Product blueprint · Proposed implementation · 10 October 2026

From customer evidence to a vehicle planning decision.

We are building a workspace where BMW product managers turn feedback and research into justified customer requirements, decide what matters, and keep those decisions current throughout a vehicle program.

Read the diagrams for the flow, use the walkthrough to follow one requirement, and open the build packages for the implementation detail. All architecture and product choices below are proposals; the BMW buyer workflow still needs validation.

Minimum record contracts

Case: program, vehicle, market, observation period, successor horizon, powertrain and configuration limits. Evidence: immutable source version, locator, original excerpt/value, applicability, translation where used. Finding: interpretation, supporting/contradicting evidence, distinct-record counts, limitations. Requirement version: customer outcome, finding links, factors/policy, evidence assessment, future assumptions, uncertainties and validation approach.

Where the PM controls the product

Review invariants we must implement

  • Approve only the exact version that the PM has inspected. Detect concurrent edits and require renewed review.
  • A change to wording, material evidence, assumptions, ratings or policy creates a proposed version. Preserve previous approvals.
  • Write the decision and its audit event in one transaction. Retried commands do not duplicate decisions.
  • Record who or what initiated the action, timestamp, reason, relevant before/after references and source versions.
  • Keep evidence strength, priority and decision status separate. High priority with weak evidence remains a validation opportunity.
  • An unresolved challenge can remain unresolved. The system can defend a proposal when the evidence supports it.

How priorities work

Start with the first plan’s four-factor rubric: customer impact, breadth in the supported target group, gap in the current offering, and future relevance. Code calculates a versioned policy. PMs inspect the judgment behind each rating.

The proposed 35/25/20/20 weights are a starting hypothesis, not a BMW standard. Compare against simpler review bands. Missing essential ratings remain incomplete. Never multiply review recurrence by sales volume to manufacture a market-demand estimate.

What sits behind the experience

Logical modules

Program and review module: scope, owners, queue, decisions, regional discussions, baselines and export.

Evidence module: source registry, adapters, locators, applicability, searches and source access.

Analysis module: validated observations, findings, requirements, scoring and bounded challenges.

Change module: dependency lookup, evidence refresh, proposed revisions and targeted notifications.

Operations module: jobs, retry/recovery, budgets, evaluation, logs and release rollback.

Integration boundary: agreed identity, source connectors and exact-version exchange with BMW tools.

These are responsibilities, not six required microservices. Start with clear module boundaries in one application and durable workers. The evidence graph can initially live in relational link tables.

Background execution and failure

Import and analysis run as persisted jobs. Save each step’s inputs, versions, output and status; retry safely after interruption. A parser or model failure produces a visible gap or review task. It must not publish malformed output or discard completed work.

Separate development/test fixtures from approved partner data. Scope source access and model inputs to the current program. Imported documents are evidence, never instructions that authorize tools or approvals.

A detailed plan with delivery gates

There is no artificial day or team limit. Each package ends in an observable outcome. More staff can parallelize supporting work, but evidence contracts, decision semantics and a real BMW workflow still form the critical path.

A Agree the BMW pilot

Suggested contributors: Product discovery + PM research. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Observe the current workflow. Record actual evidence sources, requirement artifacts, handoffs, pain points and decision authority.
  2. Choose a concrete program. Recommend G60 / US; confirm sponsor, powertrain/model-year scope, PM cohort and a successor-planning objective.
  3. Agree system ownership. Determine whether our workspace owns reviews/baselines or supplies evidence to BMW’s existing requirement system.
  4. Define the evaluation. Capture manual-task effort and quality; agree the outcomes and thresholds before seeing pilot results.
  5. Resolve access and data contracts. Confirm source permissions, deployment constraints, survey definitions and required integrations.
Deliverable and exit gate

A named design partner, an observed workflow map, a scoped pilot brief, an evidence gap list and agreed acceptance criteria.

Do not claim buyer validation before BMW confirms these decisions.

B Build the evidence foundation

Suggested contributors: Data engineering + evidence reviewers. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Feedback adapter. Normalize record ID, country and source labels; preserve repeated tags and original cell locators; separate ID counts from respondent counts.
  2. Study adapters. Parse CN/EU means and US distributions separately; retain category labels, original cells, missing N and unknown scale details.
  3. Equipment adapter. Extract model-column relationships, standard/optional symbols, package rules, exclusions, editions and page regions; route uncertain tables to review.
  4. Sales / source registry. Keep G60/G68 combined regional volumes and supplied scenario years; store immutable bytes, hashes and source access rules.
  5. Applicability and evidence viewer. Match vehicle, market, period, software and powertrain; show mismatches and unknowns; open the original source beside the extracted claim.
  6. Acquire matching baselines. Obtain US documentation for pilot facts; keep German July 2026 PDFs visibly marked as reference context.
Deliverable and exit gate

Every imported fact has a source and locator; counts reconcile; failures are visible; corpus reviewers can verify applicability.

Depends on A. No artificial joins, missing-to-zero conversion or duplicate annotation inflation.

C Deliver the full decision loop

Suggested contributors: AI/application engineering + product design + PM. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Observation extraction. Identify experienced customer tasks, pain points, valued attributes, requests and contextual constraints; validate excerpt references.
  2. Finding consolidation. Group semantic themes and distinct IDs; retain support, contradiction, uncertainty and why observations were merged.
  3. Baseline and bounded research. Check existing capability; verify material local/temporal facts; limit query budgets and preserve unresolved outcomes.
  4. Requirements and priority. Draft preserve/improve/introduce customer outcomes; expose validation criteria, future assumptions, factors, weights and incomplete ratings.
  5. PM review controls. Implement approve, reject, edit, challenge and defer; require rationale, authority and the displayed content version.
  6. Versioned audit and export. Commit mutation and audit together; reject stale approvals; make retries idempotent; export exact versions and decision status.
Deliverable and exit gate

PMs complete real tasks from evidence to decision. D1–D4 are demonstrable with actual supplied data and qualified gaps.

Depends on B. No unsupported material claim in reviewed pilot output; AI credentials cannot approve.

D Support ongoing planning

Suggested contributors: Application engineering + program/regional PMs. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Program review queue. Assign owners, due dates and review tasks; surface what needs a decision and why.
  2. Evidence impact analysis. Follow source→finding→requirement links; detect whether a new import changes material support, scope or assumptions.
  3. Controlled proposal updates. Generate a proposed successor version and concise change rationale; preserve approved content and original evidence.
  4. Program baselines. Publish snapshots for planning milestones; compare exact versions, assumptions, scores and decisions over time.
  5. Regional collaboration. Use shared need identities plus local variants; record objections, resolutions and program-level strategic choices.
  6. Notification policy. Notify affected owners rather than every PM; prioritize actionable changes and measure review burden.
Deliverable and exit gate

A new evidence batch produces the right review work; the PM can revise or retain a requirement and publish a new baseline.

Depends on C. No silent approval overwrite; duplicate imports do not create duplicate reviews.

E Prove value and production readiness

Suggested contributors: Evaluation + platform + integration engineering. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Independent quality evaluation. Use held-out BMW-reviewed examples for claim support, applicability, contradictions, consolidation and requirement usefulness.
  2. PM task comparison. Compare assisted vs existing work on matched tasks; measure time, correction burden and ability to explain decisions.
  3. Identity and permissions. Integrate approved identity; enforce program/market/source permissions server-side; review delegated roles and access events.
  4. Reliable operations. Verify interrupted-job recovery, restore, retention/deletion behavior, quotas, observability and model/prompt rollback.
  5. Native tool integration. Implement the confirmed API or export contract; preserve stable IDs, version semantics, authority and evidence links.
  6. Deployment and release policy. Agree environment and model-data rules; run regression checks before parser/prompt/model updates and staged rollout.
Deliverable and exit gate

Pilot value is demonstrated, BMW accepts the operating boundary, integration round-trips work and production controls pass agreed checks.

Requirements discovered in A and implemented throughout B–D. Dependencies include both a partner decision and technical proof.

F Expand based on demonstrated transfer

Suggested contributors: Product + data + regional reviewers. These are capabilities, not a required team size.

  1. Run secondary cohorts. Use G70 / US and F70 / GB with separately verified context; test the same contracts and review flow.
  2. Acquire missing markets. Gather local feedback and equipment for G68 / China and additional European cohorts before making coverage claims.
  3. Evaluate by cohort. Measure errors by source, vehicle, language, market and powertrain; do not hide weak groups inside an overall average.
  4. Improve models only where needed. Compare stronger retrieval, narrower prompts and specialist models; train only when held-out evaluation justifies it.
  5. Add demanded connectors. Prioritize integrations from observed PM use and repeat demand, rather than universal arbitrary file support.
Deliverable and exit gate

Every advertised cohort has applicable evidence, measured quality and a complete human decision workflow.

Depends on E for commercial rollout; small transfer checks can start earlier once C works.

The first usable release

Packages A–C produce a real evidence-to-decision pilot: reviewed source imports, findings, a structured requirement list, priority rationale, PM actions, durable versions/audit and exact-version export. Packages D–E make it a dependable commercial planning product. Package F earns broader coverage.

What we will verify before calling it ready

  • Source locators and counts match inputs; scope mismatches and unsupported claims are caught.
  • Reviewed requirements describe customer outcomes and include all required evidence, rationale, assumptions, uncertainty and decision fields.
  • PM challenges can revise, defend or leave a proposal unresolved; decisions cannot be issued by AI.
  • New evidence identifies the correct affected work while keeping earlier baselines reproducible.
  • PM task results improve against the observed manual baseline under pre-agreed criteria.
  • Approved deployment, integration, restore/recovery and access boundaries work in the intended environment.

Where we should keep options open

Choose a path below to see what changes in the build. Recommendations reflect the reviewed sources; buyer preferences, deployment constraints and quality thresholds remain unconfirmed.

Deployment: hosted, dedicated or BMW-managed?

Current position: keep the logical architecture portable. Choose after BMW confirms its environment, model access and data-handling requirements.

Hosted: simpler operation if approved. Dedicated environment: stronger operational separation with additional management. BMW-managed: fits an internal boundary but depends on its platform and access. None is assumed approved.

Import-only integration or native synchronization?

Recommendation: prove exact-version structured export first, while discovering the native integration. Use native sync when the system contract is known.

Synchronization adds conflict resolution, stable mapping, access and authority questions. If BMW’s system owns approvals, our UI must not create a competing approval truth.

Source updates: PM-triggered or scheduled?

Recommendation: start with explicit imports and owner-triggered refresh; support targeted scheduling once source rights, freshness needs and notification usefulness are clear.

Neither option is allowed to update an approved requirement automatically. Both create proposed changes and review work. The missing dates in supplied feedback limit longitudinal conclusions.

Weighted score or ordinal review bands?

Recommendation: test the four-factor rubric alongside simple high/medium/lower review bands with real PMs.

Scores reveal factor tradeoffs but can imply false precision. Bands are easier to use but less granular. Choose by reviewer agreement, usefulness and sensitivity; store every policy version.

One model, multiple specialists or fine-tuning?

Recommendation: one shared validated workflow first; evaluate candidate models on held-out partner-reviewed tasks. Improve retrieval and task contracts before adding training.

Use a specialist when it measurably improves a specific task. Fine-tuning needs authorized labeled examples and independent evaluation; the supplied workbook tags are not a trustworthy training target by themselves.

Relational links or a separate graph database?

Recommendation: relational links for initial evidence lineage and impact queries. Add a graph system only when measured traversal/query needs justify another operational system.

Human review of every extraction or exceptions only?

Recommendation: review uncertain/material extraction and maintain an independent quality sample. PM decisions remain mandatory for consequential requirement acceptance.

The exception thresholds must come from observed error rates and partner tolerance. Confidence generated by a model alone is not sufficient to skip review.

Discovery decisions that change the plan

The BMW sponsor, actual requirement tool, decision authority, deployment rules, source metadata, survey method, matching equipment and pilot success thresholds must be confirmed. Keep these as named decisions with owners and due gates, rather than hiding assumptions inside prompts or code.
